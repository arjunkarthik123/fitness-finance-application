\section{General Information}

\subsection{Summary}

The software being tested is \progname{}, an app that allows users to learn about the power of compounding through working out and investing.
The team will be looking to test the following functions of the app:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Step tracking and integrations with fitness data sources (Android Health Connect and, optionally, Garmin watches)
  \item Real-time updates of fitness data within the app
  \item Converting fitness progress into virtual currency and depositing it into the user's account
  \item Viewing account balances
  \item Simulated/generated financial markets
  \item Performance leaderboards
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Objectives}

The main objective of the VnV plan is to ensure that \progname{} properly teaches the impact of compounding.
For the VnV plan to achieve this objective, the team will prioritize the following qualities:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Learnability:} The app is easy for new users to learn, as measured by the learnability requirement in the SRS (UHR-EOU-08).
  \item \textbf{Correctness of currency calculations:} Virtual currency earned, deposited, and displayed in account balances is calculated correctly, and operations that change balances either complete fully or are rolled back (PR-RFT-02).
  \item \textbf{Consistency of asset calculations:} Simulated asset values are consistent and produce the returns expected from the implemented market algorithms, including the calibration against historical market data required by PR-PA-06.
  \item \textbf{User confidence in the financial lessons:} Users come away trusting and understanding the financial lessons the app is intended to convey.
\end{itemize}

The following objectives are out of scope for this VnV plan:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Assumptions in the SRS and Hazard Analysis:} The assumptions listed in the SRS \citep{SRS} and the Critical Assumptions section of the Hazard Analysis are taken as given and will not be verified.
  \item \textbf{Third-party packages and libraries:} External dependencies, such as Android Health Connect and the Garmin Connect IQ SDK, are assumed to be correct and already verified by their implementation teams (e.g.\ Garmin). The team does not have the time or resources to verify these libraries within the scope of this capstone. This matches the Hazard Analysis assumption that third-party health platforms keep their documented behaviour. \progname{}'s own handling of outages and malformed data from these sources is still in scope (PR-RFT-03).
  \item \textbf{Internet connectivity:} The SRS lists a mobile data or Wi-Fi connection as part of the environment needed to reach the \progname{} backend. The strength and reliability of the user's connection will not be tested. The app's required offline behaviour (PR-RFT-01 and PR-RFT-04) is still in scope.
  \item \textbf{Full verification of user-reported fitness activity:} The team will not attempt to fully verify that users are not lying about completing fitness goals, as this would require too much time and resources. The SRS already assumes that fitness devices report correct data and that artificial changes are dishonest use, and SC-04 limits this risk by not allowing manual entry of activity. Small checks may be used to gauge validity in some cases (e.g.\ checking that a Garmin watch's heart rate reading stays above a threshold for a period of time), but validity cannot be guaranteed, especially for users without a Garmin device who track activity solely through their phone.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Custom Documents}

The team is completing two custom documents (CDs), one per term:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{CD-Fall --- User/Stakeholder/Client Interview Report:} A report on interviews with the project's client and potential users, used to elicit requirements. The SRS appendix already summarizes the two interviews conducted so far: a mock interview with the client and supervisor, and an interview with a representative end user.
  \item \textbf{CD-Winter --- Business Plan:} A business plan exploring how \progname{} could be monetized after the capstone, as the team believes the project has the potential to become a startup. It will cover the market need, market size and business model, costs, competition, and an execution plan.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relevant Documentation}

The following documents are relevant to the VnV efforts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Software Requirements Specification (SRS)} \citep{SRS}: Defines the functional and non-functional requirements of \progname{}, each with a fit criterion. These fit criteria are what the tests in this plan check against. The SRS also lists the assumptions that bound what is in scope for verification, and names the testing tools the team will use (JUnit for backend unit and system tests, and Detox for end-to-end tests of the mobile client).
  \item \textbf{Hazard Analysis:} Identifies the hazards of using \progname{} and defines twelve safety and security requirements (SR1--SR12) to mitigate them, each with a fit criterion. Its roadmap commits to implementing SR1, SR2, SR3, SR6, SR8, SR9, SR11, and SR12 within the capstone, so these are the safety and security requirements this plan verifies. Its Critical Assumptions are excluded from verification.
  \item \textbf{Health Connect and Garmin Connect IQ SDK documentation:} Describes how activity data is provided to \progname{}. Since these platforms are assumed to keep their documented behaviour, their documentation defines the data the integration is expected to receive.
  \item \textbf{Custom Documents:} The interview report (CD-Fall) records the user and client needs that the requirements were derived from, which is what validation testing checks the app against. The business plan (CD-Winter) has little direct bearing on VnV, but one SRS scalability requirement refers to it for how the product might grow after the capstone.
  \item \textbf{User interviews and user testing:} The interviews in the SRS appendix ground the requirements in real user needs. The SRS also plans user testing on demo builds in Term 2 (Weeks 17 and 21), which will be used to assess learnability and user confidence in the financial lessons.
\end{itemize}

\section{Plan}

This section outlines the plan to verify and validate Flexin' across its project development lifecycle.
The plan will detail the roles and responsibilities of the team, and will specify the approach to verify the SRS document, design and implementation, as well as how to verify the VnV itself.
Lastly, it will describe the automated tools and software validation framework that the team plans on using.

\subsection{SRS Verification}

The SRS will be verified to ensure that all requirements detailed in the document are reasonable, accurate, understandable and complete.
The bullet list below describes the different avenues in which the SRS may be reviewed.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Submission to TA:} The SRS will be submitted to our course TA, who will thoroughly read and analyze the document and provide feedback where needed.
  \item \textbf{Peer Review:} Classmates will review the SRS document and ensure its content makes sense from a course standpoint.
  \item \textbf{Presentation to Supervisor:} The team may meet with the project supervisor to ensure that the requirements described in the SRS are high quality and in line with what they expect to be delivered.
\end{itemize}

If any problem are found with the SRS, the team will utilize our issue board to keep track and fix them.
Once development begins, these issues may also coincide with a bugfix.

\subsection{Design Verification}

Design verification will begin after the submission of the Design Document on November 16th.
This verification will ensure that Flexin's architecture meets requirements and is implementable.
The bullet list below describes how the Design Document may be reviewed.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Submission to TA:} The Design Document will be submitted to our course TA, who will thoroughly read and analyze the document and provide feedback where needed.
  \item \textbf{Peer Review:} Classmates will review the Design document and ensure its content makes sense from a course standpoint.
  \item \textbf{Presentation to Supervisor:} The team may meet with the project supervisor to ensure that the design is feasible from an implementation point of view.
\end{itemize}

Any problems with the Design Document will be handled through the teams issue board.


\subsection{Verification and Validation Plan Verification}

VnV plan verification will take place before the document is submitted by the team, as well as post submission by other members.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Team Review:} The team will review the VnV Plan ensuring that the defined tasks are feasible within the timeframe of the course and that the detail described in the document is appropriate.
  \item \textbf{Peer Review:} Classmates will review the VnV Plan and ensure its content makes sense from a course standpoint.
  \item \textbf{Presentation to Supervisor:} The team may meet with the project supervisor to ensure that the VnV Plan is feasible.
  \item \textbf{Submission to TA:} The VnV Plan will be submitted to the TA for grading and feedback.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implementation Verification}

Implementation verification ensures that the system, as built, correctly implements the SRS and design document.
This will be done through a combination of the following:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unit Testing and System Testing:} All functional and non-functional requirements will be verified through the tests described in the below sections of this document.
  \item \textbf{Static Analysis and Linting:} The team will enforce a coding standard using linters and static analyzers.
  \item \textbf{Code Review:} All code changes will require a pull request and approval from at least one team member.
  The team members will be looking for code correctness, standards, and proper error handling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Automated Testing and Verification Tools}

The team plans on creating standard unit tests that will run during the CI stage of the code development lifecycle.
The framework used for the backend will most likely be JUnit, and for the frontend, Detox.
The team may also create integration tests and end-to-end tests to ensure that application functionality doesn't degrade in the grand scheme.
Code coverage will be top of mind while developing this test plan, and a tool to ensure that a minimum agreed threshold of 80\% coverage will be implemented.

\subsection{Software Validation}

The software validation step of the VnV plan will ensure that the near-final product lives up to its expectations.
This validation will be done from the perspective of the users and the stakeholders.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rev-0 Demonstration:} The Rev-0 demo will be used to ensure that the core functionality of Flexin' is up to the standards of what the supervisor expects.
  \item \textbf{Usability Tests:} The team plans on giving Flexin' to a select group of prospective users for a period of roughly 2 weeks.
  This will help the team understand what needs work, and what worked well for the users.
\end{itemize}
