\section{Purpose of the Project}
This section outlines the user business context and the overarching project
goals for the \progname{} application.

\subsection{User Business}
Personal finance and physical fitness both depend on the principle of
compounding, where consistent, early actions yield substantial long-term
benefits; however, young adults often neglect both disciplines due to delayed
gratification and widespread gaps in financial literacy. While existing software
tools address workout tracking and stock market simulations independently, none
connect daily physical activity directly to experiential investing. The business
context of \progname{} centers on bridging this gap by using healthy,
habit-building fitness routines as the gateway to practical financial education,
as detailed in the
\href{https://github.com/arjunkarthik123/fitness-finance-application/blob/main/pdfs/ProblemStatementAndGoals/ProblemStatement.pdf}{Problem Statement and Goals}
and \href{https://github.com/arjunkarthik123/fitness-finance-application/blob/main/pdfs/DevelopmentPlan/DevelopmentPlan.pdf}{Development Plan}.

\subsection{Goals of the Project}
The primary goal of \progname{} is to teach users the power of compounding by
rewarding tracked physical activity with virtual currency that can be invested
in a simulated financial market of fictional stocks, bonds, and \acr{ETF}{exchange-traded funds}{ETFs} whose
prices follow real-world market trends. In
addition, the project aims to integrate seamlessly with existing Android health
technologies and wearable devices, deliver an intuitive user experience
accessible to beginners, and drive sustained user engagement to cultivate lasting wellness and investing habits. Further
details regarding project scheduling and project milestones are
outlined in the
\href{https://github.com/arjunkarthik123/fitness-finance-application/blob/main/pdfs/DevelopmentPlan/DevelopmentPlan.pdf}{Development Plan}
and \href{https://github.com/arjunkarthik123/fitness-finance-application/blob/main/pdfs/ProblemStatementAndGoals/ProblemStatement.pdf}{Problem Statement and Goals}.

\subsubsection{Conventions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item ``Shall'' marks a mandatory requirement. Requirements are written to be
  black-box: they say what the product must do, not how. Design decisions that
  are imposed on the team are recorded as solution constraints (\hyperlink{req:SC-01}{SC-01} to \hyperlink{req:SC-06}{SC-06}).
  \item Every requirement has a unique ID. IDs use the prefixes in
  Table~\ref{tab:id-prefixes} followed by a two-digit number. Every mention of
  a requirement ID is a hyperlink to its definition, and the traceability
  matrix lists, for each requirement, every requirement it relates to in both
  directions.
  \item Values that appear in several requirements, or that the team will tune,
  are symbolic constants. They are written in \textsf{sans-serif}, link to
  Table~\ref{tab:constants}, and are defined only there.
  \item References such as SR8 and H4.1 refer to a
  \acr{SR}{safety requirement}{SR} and a hazard in the Hazard Analysis.
  \item Amounts of virtual currency use the \acr{FLX}{virtual currency symbol}{FLX}. Days, dates, and times
  of day are in the user's local time zone.
  \item Acronyms are spelled out at first use and are listed in the Glossary
  (Section~\ref{sec:glossary}) with a link to where each is defined.
  \acr{NA}{Not applicable}{N/A} marks template sections that do not apply to this project.
\end{itemize}

The client with whom the team is building the app is \textbf{Chris Schankula}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primary}: Young adults aged 18-30 who are seeking to build financial literacy and sustainable fitness habits. 
    \item \textbf{Secondary:} Adults older than the intended age range who are looking to learn new skills, reinforce or start new healthy habits.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Financial Institution/Banks:} which benefit from financially literate consumers who will engage more with their finances.

\subsection{Hands-On Users of the Project}
\label{sec:user-characteristics}
The main users of the app will tend to share the following traits and goals across both the finance and the fitness domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Age:} adults (MinAge or older), mostly 18--30, including university students and people early in their careers.

    \item \textbf{Language and location:} live in Canada and use the app in English or French.

    \item \textbf{Accessibility:} some users have visual, hearing, or motor impairments, or colour vision deficiency.

    \item \textbf{Motivation:} respond to goals, streaks, and friendly competition, but can be discouraged by repeated failure or by comparisons with others.

    \item \textbf{Financial knowledge:} most users are beginners who want to learn more about personal finance and build confidence in managing money. Others already have a stronger foundation and are looking for deeper guidance or new ways to challenge themselves and improve their financial decision-making.

    \item \textbf{Fitness level:} ranges from individuals just beginning their fitness journey to those who exercise regularly. Some users aim to learn proper techniques or build consistency, while others seek new challenges to push their performance further. Certain users may also have injuries or conditions that influence the types of activities they can safely perform.

    \item \textbf{Mobile (Android) Smartphone Users:} users will interact with the app and utilize its features to view and track both financial and fitness goals. For the purpose of Capstone, the final deployed app will be for Android users only.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mandated Constraints}
\subsection{Solution Constraints}

The constraints below limit how the product can be built. They come from
decisions recorded in the Problem Statement and the Development Plan, and the
design must satisfy them.

\begin{description}
  \item[{SC-01\hypertarget{req:SC-01}{}}: Android platform]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The product shall run as a native mobile application
    on Android phones.
    \item[Rationale:] The team narrowed the scope from multiple operating
    systems to Android only so the product can be finished to a high standard
    within the capstone timeline. Support for iOS and other platforms is a
    stretch goal.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] The application installs and runs all core features
    on an Android phone.
    \item[Related Requirements:] \hyperlink{req:MAS-ADPT-02}{MAS-ADPT-02}
  \end{description}

  \item[{SC-02\hypertarget{req:SC-02}{}}: Frontend technology]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The mobile client shall be written in TypeScript using
    the React Native framework.
    \item[Rationale:] The team agreed on this stack in the Development Plan,
    based on the members' existing experience. React Native also leaves room
    for an iOS build later without rewriting the client.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] All client source code is TypeScript built on React
    Native.
    \item[Related Requirements:] \hyperlink{req:COMP-LEG-07}{COMP-LEG-07}, \hyperlink{req:COMP-STD-03}{COMP-STD-03}
  \end{description}

  \item[{SC-03\hypertarget{req:SC-03}{}}: Backend technology]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The backend \acr{API}{application programming interface}{API} shall be built in Java with the Spring
    framework, and persistent data shall be stored in a MySQL relational
    database.
    \item[Rationale:] Chosen in the Development Plan. A relational database
    keeps user, transaction, and portfolio records structured and quick to
    query.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] The API is a Spring application and all persistent
    application data lives in a MySQL database.
    \item[Related Requirements:] \hyperlink{req:COMP-LEG-07}{COMP-LEG-07}, \hyperlink{req:COMP-STD-04}{COMP-STD-04}, \hyperlink{req:COMP-STD-05}{COMP-STD-05}
  \end{description}

  \item[{SC-04\hypertarget{req:SC-04}{}}: Fitness data sources]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] Activity data shall be read automatically from the
    phone's built-in health data. Data from external devices such as fitness
    watches or heart rate monitors shall only be read through a \acr{SDK}{software development kit}{SDK} or API
    published by the device's manufacturer.
    \item[Rationale:] Automatic tracking avoids manual entry, which users could
    inflate to earn currency. Using official manufacturer interfaces keeps the
    integration supported and within the manufacturer's terms of use.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] The product has no feature for manually entering step
    counts, and every external device integration uses a documented SDK or API
    from that device's manufacturer.
    \item[Related Requirements:] \hyperlink{req:FUNC-03}{FUNC-03}, \hyperlink{req:COMP-LEG-07}{COMP-LEG-07}
  \end{description}

  \item[{SC-05\hypertarget{req:SC-05}{}}: Simulated market only]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] All trading shall take place in a simulated market
    using virtual currency. The product shall not handle real money, connect
    to a brokerage, or let users convert virtual currency into cash.
    \item[Rationale:] The goal is to teach how investments grow, not to run a
    trading platform. Handling real money or real trades would bring financial
    regulation that is outside the scope of a capstone project.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] No feature of the product accepts, stores, or pays
    out real money, and no trade is sent to a real exchange or broker.
    \item[Related Requirements:] \hyperlink{req:FUNC-09}{FUNC-09}, \hyperlink{req:FUNC-10}{FUNC-10}, \hyperlink{req:FUNC-17}{FUNC-17}
  \end{description}

  \item[{SC-06\hypertarget{req:SC-06}{}}: Distribution]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The product shall be distributed to users through the
    Android application store.
    \item[Rationale:] The Problem Statement specifies that distribution is
    handled by the device's application store, which also handles installs and
    updates.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] The release build meets the store's publishing
    requirements and can be installed from it.
    \item[Related Requirements:] \hyperlink{req:PROD-01}{PROD-01}, \hyperlink{req:COMP-STD-01}{COMP-STD-01}
  \end{description}
\end{description}
\subsection{Implementation Environment of the Current System}

\progname{} is a new product and does not replace an existing system. Today,
people who want to build both habits use separate tools: a fitness tracker or
the phone's own health app for activity, and a stock market simulator or a real
brokerage for investing. Nothing links the two.

The product will run on the environment its users already have:

\begin{itemize}
  \item An Android phone with Health Connect, which collects activity data from
  the phone's sensors and from other fitness apps the user has installed.
  \item Optionally, a Garmin watch.
  \item A mobile data or Wi-Fi connection to reach the \progname{} backend.
\end{itemize}

The MySQL database (see \hyperlink{req:SC-03}{SC-03}) will be hosted on McMaster University's MySQL
server, provided to the team for the course.

\subsection{Partner or Collaborative Applications}

The product depends on the following external applications for fitness data.
Each integration must follow the provider's developer terms and permission
model (see \hyperlink{req:SC-04}{SC-04}).

\begin{description}
  \item[Health Connect (Android):] The main source of step counts and workout
  data. The user must grant \progname{} permission to read each data type.
  \item[Garmin Connect IQ:] Gives access to activity data from Garmin watches
  for users who own one.
\end{description}

The simulated market does not depend on any external price feed. Its assets and
prices are fictional and are generated by the product's own market engine (see
The Context of the Work). To make price movements follow real-world trends, the
engine is calibrated against historical market data (see \hyperlink{req:PR-PA-06}{PR-PA-06}). Any
historical data set used for this must be free to use in an educational
application.

\subsection{Anticipated Workplace Environment}

\progname{} is used on the user's phone, wherever they are. That affects
the design in a few ways:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Users will often check the app in short sessions, for example after a
  walk or workout, while outdoors, or while moving. Key screens need to be
  readable on a small screen in bright light and usable with one hand.
  \item The connection may be slow or drop out. Activity data must not be lost
  when the phone is offline; it should sync once the connection returns.
  \item Users should not need a manual or formal training to use the app (see
  \hyperlink{req:UHR-LRN-01}{UHR-LRN-01}). The interface has to explain financial terms itself, in line with the goal of a simple and delightful user
  experience.
  \item Step counting runs in the background through Health Connect, so the
  app must not drain the battery noticeably during a normal day.
\end{itemize}

\section{Relevant Facts And Assumptions}
\subsection{Relevant Facts}

\begin{itemize} 
  \item Physical fitness and financial investing both rely on the principle of compounding, where small and consistent actions can produce significant long-term outcomes. 
  \item Financial literacy among young adults remains a challenge, and many individuals lack confidence in making investment decisions without additional education. 
  \item Existing fitness applications and investment simulators generally operate independently and do not directly connect physical activity with financial education. 
  \item Modern Android devices are capable of collecting activity information through Health Connect and other supported fitness integrations. 
  \item Users are increasingly familiar with gamified mobile experiences that use rewards, achievements, streaks, and leaderboards to encourage continued engagement. 
  \item Users may possess significantly different levels of financial knowledge, fitness experience, and technological proficiency. 
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Business Rules}

\begin{itemize} 
  \item Only validated fitness activity may be used to generate virtual currency rewards. 
  \item Virtual currency has no real-world monetary value and cannot be exchanged for money, goods, or services. Exchanging currency for real-world rewards from sponsors is a stretch goal (see the Waiting Room).
  \item Virtual currency is earned only through physical activity, and a user's balance grows otherwise only through the returns on their simulated investments.
  \item All investment activity shall occur within a simulated market environment. 
  \item Users must possess sufficient virtual currency before a simulated purchase can be executed. 
  \item Users may only sell assets that they currently own within their simulated portfolio. 
  \item Participation in social and competitive features shall be optional. 
  \item Users retain ownership and control over their personal information and may request account deletion in accordance with applicable privacy regulations. 
  \item Educational content provided by the application is intended for informational purposes only and shall not constitute financial advice. 
  \item Fitness rewards shall be earned through physical activity and not through direct purchases or monetary contributions. 
  \item Community rankings shall only include users who have elected to participate. 
  \item User-generated content must comply with the application's community standards and may not contain offensive, abusive, discriminatory, or harassing material. 
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Assumptions}
The following assumptions will be made during the process of development:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fitness Devices Properly Track Workouts}: Flexin' will assume that data provided by fitness devices is correct and only changed through the intended means. Any artificial changes will be deemed as dishonest use and Flexin' will not provide value.
    \item \textbf{The User's Device Is Not Compromised}: Flexin' assumes that the device running the app has not been rooted, "jailbroken", or infected with malware. 
    \item \textbf{The University Database Server Provides Its Advertised Guarantees}: Flexin' assumes that McMaster University's MySQL server, which hosts its database, meets its stated availability, durability, and security guarantees, so that private information is not put at risk by the server itself.
    \item \textbf{Users Provide Truthful Personal Information}: Flexin' assumes that the height, weight, fitness level, and health conditions users enter are accurate to the best of their knowledge.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Scope of the Work}
\subsection{The Current Situation}

The modern software landscape treats physical health management and
financial education as separate domains, despite both relying on
consistent daily actions and the long-term benefits of compounding.
Existing fitness applications, such as Strava, Garmin Connect, and
Google Fit, track physical activity through metrics such as steps,
heart rate, and \acr{GPS}{Global Positioning System}{GPS} routes, while using badges, streaks, and milestones
to encourage engagement. However, the long-term effectiveness of these
incentives varies, and meaningful health benefits often require
sustained participation over time.

Similarly, financial simulators provide users with risk-free investment
experiences but may rely on virtual capital that is disconnected from
real-world effort and financial constraints. These environments can
allow users to experiment with investing without experiencing the
consequences of actual financial decisions. Furthermore, their
educational value depends on users' motivation to learn, rather than
providing a direct incentive to develop stronger personal financial
management habits.

Financial literacy remains a significant challenge in Canada.
Boisclair et al. found that only 42\% of Canadian respondents correctly
answered three foundational questions concerning compound interest,
inflation, and risk diversification~\cite{boisclair2017}.
The same study found that respondents who answered all three questions
correctly were 10 percentage points more likely to have retirement
savings, demonstrating an association between financial knowledge and
retirement preparedness~\cite{boisclair2017}.

Despite their shared emphasis on consistency and compounding, physical
activity tracking and financial education remain largely disconnected.
Fitness data is primarily managed through health-focused platforms,
while investment simulators do not typically connect simulated capital
to users' real-world effort or habit formation. Consequently, users
lack an integrated way to explore how small, repeated actions can
contribute to long-term outcomes across both domains.

This gap presents an opportunity for an integrated platform that
connects physical activity with simulated investing. By linking
consistent activity to incremental virtual investment opportunities,
such a platform could introduce more tangible short-term incentives,
demonstrate the effects of compounding, and teach financial concepts
through familiar daily habits. This approach could encourage sustained
participation while helping users develop the financial literacy
needed for informed long-term decision-making.


\subsection{The Context of the Work}

The Flexin' system connects physical activity tracking with simulated
investing by converting validated exercise data into virtual investment
capital. Users can use this capital to build simulated portfolios
containing fictional stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), funds, bonds,
and other supported assets without risking real money. Flexin' integrates
with Android Health Connect and Garmin to retrieve supported fitness
data, while its internally developed financial market engine generates
fictional assets, prices, and market events. The system also provides
portfolio management, financial education, and notifications.

\subsubsection{Information Flows Across the Work Boundary}

\paragraph{Inbound Information.}
Inbound information includes user commands, profile and configuration
data, exercise goals, and simulated trade orders. Flexin' also receives
fitness telemetry, such as step counts and workout records, through
Android Health Connect and the Garmin integration. The custom financial
market engine supplies internally generated asset prices, historical
price series, simulated market events, and distributions such as
dividends or fund returns.

\paragraph{Outbound Information.}
Outbound information includes virtual currency earned through validated
activity, portfolio balances, asset allocations, and investment
performance visualizations. Flexin' also provides contextual financial
education on compound growth, diversification, and investment risk.
Optional leaderboards, activity milestones, workout reminders, and
simulated market notifications encourage continued engagement.

\subsubsection{Boundary Scope and Enclosure}

\paragraph{In Scope.}
Flexin' includes fitness data validation, activity-to-currency
conversion, duplicate reward prevention, simulated portfolio
management, and virtual trade execution. Its custom financial market
engine generates fictional assets and determines their simulated
prices and performance. The backend manages accounts, asset positions,
transaction records, and portfolio valuations, while the React Native
and TypeScript frontend presents fitness and investment dashboards.
An educational rules engine provides contextual explanations of
financial concepts.

\paragraph{Out of Scope.}
Flexin' does not depend on real-world stock prices, execute actual
financial transactions, or manage real investment funds. It does not
control the accuracy or availability of third-party fitness data,
Garmin services, Android Health Connect, or device hardware. External
fitness integrations remain subject to provider capabilities, API
availability, device compatibility, and user permissions.

\subsection{Specifying a Business Use Case (BUC)}

This section specifies each Flexin' Business Use Case (BUC), including
its goal, actors, trigger, preconditions, main flow, alternative flows,
and postconditions.

\subsubsection*{BUC 1: User Onboarding}

\textbf{Goal:} Create a user account and initialize their Flexin' profile.

\textbf{Actors:} End User.

\textbf{Trigger:} The user opens Flexin' and selects the registration option.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The application is available and the user does not
already have an active account.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The user submits the required registration details.
    \item Flexin' validates the details and creates the account.
    \item The user provides any requested baseline goals or preferences.
    \item Flexin' initializes the user's profile and virtual wallet.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If required details are missing or invalid,
Flexin' displays an error and requests corrections. If an account already
exists, the user is prompted to sign in.

\textbf{Postconditions:} The user account and initial virtual wallet are
created successfully.

\subsubsection*{BUC 2: Activity Ingestion}

\textbf{Goal:} Retrieve and validate fitness activity data from supported
integrations.

\textbf{Actors:} End User, Android Health Connect, Garmin Integration.

\textbf{Trigger:} New activity data becomes available or the user initiates
an activity data synchronization.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The user is registered and has granted the required
permissions to an available supported integration.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Flexin' retrieves available activity data from Health Connect or Garmin.
    \item Flexin' validates the data and checks for duplicate activities.
    \item Valid activities are stored in the user's activity history.
    \item The application updates the user's activity summary.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If permissions are missing, the user is prompted
to grant access. If an integration is unavailable or data is invalid,
Flexin' reports the issue without recording the invalid activity.

\textbf{Postconditions:} Valid activity data is stored without duplicate
records.

\subsubsection*{BUC 3: Currency Allocation}

\textbf{Goal:} Reward eligible physical activity with virtual currency.

\textbf{Actors:} End User, Flexin' Reward System.

\textbf{Trigger:} A validated activity satisfies a configured reward threshold.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The user has an active account and the activity has
been validated.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Flexin' evaluates the activity against the reward rules.
    \item The system calculates the eligible virtual currency reward.
    \item Flexin' credits the user's virtual wallet and records the transaction.
    \item The user can view the updated balance and reward details.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If the activity does not meet the reward
threshold, no currency is awarded. If the activity has already been
rewarded, Flexin' prevents duplicate crediting.

\textbf{Postconditions:} The wallet balance and transaction ledger reflect
any eligible reward.

\subsubsection*{BUC 4: Asset Purchase}

\textbf{Goal:} Allow the user to purchase fictional financial assets using
virtual currency.

\textbf{Actors:} End User, Flexin' Market Engine.

\textbf{Trigger:} The user submits a simulated buy order.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The user is registered, the selected asset exists,
and a valid simulated price is available.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The user selects an asset and enters the purchase quantity.
    \item Flexin' calculates the simulated purchase cost.
    \item The system verifies that the user has sufficient virtual currency.
    \item Flexin' executes the simulated order and updates the wallet and holdings.
    \item The completed transaction is recorded and displayed to the user.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If the balance is insufficient, the order is
rejected. If the asset or price is unavailable, the user is informed and
the transaction is not executed.

\textbf{Postconditions:} For a successful purchase, the virtual balance is
reduced and the user's asset holdings increase accordingly.

\subsubsection*{BUC 5: Asset Sale}

\textbf{Goal:} Allow the user to sell fictional assets and receive virtual
currency.

\textbf{Actors:} End User, Flexin' Market Engine.

\textbf{Trigger:} The user submits a simulated sell order.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The user holds a sufficient quantity of the selected
asset and a valid simulated price is available.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The user selects an asset and enters the quantity to sell.
    \item Flexin' calculates the simulated sale proceeds.
    \item The system verifies that the user holds sufficient units.
    \item Flexin' executes the simulated order and updates the holdings and wallet.
    \item The completed transaction is recorded and displayed to the user.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If the user holds insufficient units or the
asset price is unavailable, the order is rejected without changing the
portfolio.

\textbf{Postconditions:} For a successful sale, the user's holdings decrease
and the corresponding virtual currency is credited.

\subsubsection*{BUC 6: Market Simulation}

\textbf{Goal:} Generate and update fictional asset prices and market events.

\textbf{Actors:} Flexin' Market Engine, Flexin' Scheduler.

\textbf{Trigger:} A scheduled market simulation cycle begins.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The market engine is operational and fictional assets
have been initialized.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The scheduler initiates a simulation cycle.
    \item The engine generates price changes and fictional market events
    according to the system's simulation rules.
    \item Flexin' updates asset valuations and relevant market information.
    \item Updated prices and events become available to portfolio and trading
    functions.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If a simulation cycle fails, Flexin' records the
failure and retains the last valid market state until an update succeeds.

\textbf{Postconditions:} The latest valid fictional asset prices and market
events are available within Flexin'. The engine does not rely on real-world
financial market data.

\subsubsection*{BUC 7: Portfolio Analytics}

\textbf{Goal:} Present the user with portfolio performance and activity-related
progress.

\textbf{Actors:} End User.

\textbf{Trigger:} The user opens the portfolio analytics view.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The user is registered and portfolio or activity data
is available.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Flexin' retrieves the user's virtual balance, holdings, and transaction
    history.
    \item The system calculates portfolio value and performance metrics using
    simulated asset prices.
    \item Flexin' retrieves relevant activity and reward information.
    \item The application displays the resulting portfolio and activity summaries.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If insufficient history exists, Flexin' displays
available information and indicates that more data is needed for certain
metrics.

\textbf{Postconditions:} The user can view their latest available portfolio
and activity analytics.

\subsubsection*{BUC 8: Financial Education}

\textbf{Goal:} Provide educational guidance relevant to the user's simulated
investing activity.

\textbf{Actors:} End User, Flexin' Educational Rules Engine.

\textbf{Trigger:} The user requests financial guidance or opens an educational
feature.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The user can access the application.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Flexin' identifies the context of the user's request.
    \item The educational rules engine selects relevant educational content.
    \item Flexin' presents explanations or guidance to the user.
    \item The user reviews the content and may return to the application.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If no specific guidance matches the request,
Flexin' presents general educational content.

\textbf{Postconditions:} Relevant educational content is displayed. Guidance
is educational and does not execute trades on the user's behalf.

\subsubsection*{BUC 9: Community Leaderboard}

\textbf{Goal:} Allow users who have opted in to a leaderboard group to view
rankings based on progress towards their own goals or the group's agreed
goals.

\textbf{Actors:} End User.

\textbf{Trigger:} The user opens the community leaderboard.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The user has access to Flexin' and has enabled any
participation required for their own information to appear in rankings.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Flexin' retrieves eligible ranking data from participating users.
    \item The system calculates or retrieves rankings using the configured metrics.
    \item The application displays the leaderboard and its ranking criteria.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If the user has opted out or no ranking data is
available, Flexin' displays the available leaderboard information without
including the user's opted-out data.

\textbf{Postconditions:} The user can view the available community rankings
in accordance with participation and privacy settings.

\subsubsection*{BUC 10: Notifications}

\textbf{Goal:} Notify users about relevant activity rewards, reminders, and
simulated market events.

\textbf{Actors:} End User, Flexin' Notification Service, Android Device and Operating System.

\textbf{Trigger:} A configured reminder, eligible reward, or relevant
simulated market event occurs.

\textbf{Preconditions:} The relevant event or schedule exists, and the user
has enabled the applicable notifications and granted any required device
permissions.

\textbf{Main Flow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Flexin' identifies the event and determines whether a notification
    should be sent.
    \item The system generates a notification containing relevant information.
    \item Flexin' sends the notification through the supported device mechanism.
    \item The user receives the notification if device settings and permissions
    permit delivery.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Alternative Flows:} If notifications are disabled or delivery fails,
Flexin' does not guarantee delivery and may retain the event for in-app display.

\textbf{Postconditions:} The notification is submitted for delivery or the
delivery failure is handled according to the system's notification rules.

\section{The Scope of the Product}
This section will explore the different features of the application. This section explores the full scope of Flexin' application and will help to define main user functions.
\subsection{Product Boundary}

\subsection*{App Functionality}
The Flexin' application is designed as an educational tool to help young adults improve their personal fitness and financial literacy. The app provides a range of interactive features that allow users to engage with both domains in a meaningful and motivating way.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Create a personal profile within the application. This profile allows users to:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Enter personal information to tailor and control fitness goals.
        \item Select the desired level of difficulty and pace for financial learning modules.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Enter, manage, and track fitness goals.
    \item Complete fitness challenges to earn virtual currency.
    \item Use virtual currency to invest in a simulated stock market environment to practice financial principles.
    \item Opt in to leaderboard groups that rank members by their progress towards their own goals or the group's agreed goals.
\end{itemize}
\subsection*{Excluded Functionality / Outside Scope}
The Flexin' application will be developed specifically for the Android operating system and optimized for use within Canada. Support for other operating systems or international deployment is outside the scope of this project.
\subsection*{External Systems}
To accurately track fitness goals, the application must retrieve data such as steps taken, heart rate, calories burned, and distance traveled. Modern Android devices provide these metrics natively, and the app will integrate with the Android Health Connect API to access this information.
Additionally, many external fitness devices (e.g., Garmin watches) offer APIs and SDKs that provide detailed activity data. The Flexin' app will communicate with these external systems to retrieve and incorporate user fitness data where supported.

UHR-EOU-01\hypertarget{req:UHR-EOU-01}{} & 
Onboarding Completion Time & 
\textbf{Description:} The system shall enable new users to complete initial account setup and onboarding within 5 minutes. 

\textbf{Rationale:} Users should be able to begin using the application almost immediately without excessive setup requirements. Within 5 minutes allows users to maintain their engagement with the system. If the setup and onboarding take too long, users may become frustrated and disengaged. 

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A first-time user shall be able to setup their account, complete onboarding and reach the main dashboard/screen within 5 minutes. This will be tested later in the project through user testing and observation.

Dashboard Accessibility & 
\textbf{Description:} The system shall provide direct access to fitness progress, portfolio information, and rewards through the main dashboard.

\textbf{Rationale:} Users should not need to navigate through multiple screens to access frequently used information. 

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Important high-level \acr{KPI}{key performance indicators}{KPIs} pertaining to fitness progress, portfolio value, and virtual currency balance shall be visible from the application's primary screen after login. Users should be able to answer questions like "How much currency do I have available?", "What percentage of my fitness goal is complete?" and "How is my portfolio performing?" within 1 minute after logging into the system.

Minimal Screen Navigation & 
\textbf{Description:} The system shall minimize navigation complexity by ensuring that portfolio management and lower-level metric viewing can be reached from the main dashboard within two taps.

\textbf{Rationale:} Users should not need to navigate through multiple screens to access detailed metrics and adjust their portfolios. Excessive navigation increases user frustration and decreases engagement. 

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Users shall be able to access detailed fitness statistics, portfolio management, virtual currency transactions, and goal tracking within two taps from the main dashboard.

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:LF-APP-05}{LF-APP-05}\\

\textbf{Description:} The system shall provide clear, actionable, and immediate feedback to the user whenever a user action cannot be completed, including failed account creation and sign-in.

\textbf{Rationale:} Clear feedback helps users understand how to resolve issues and continue using the application, instead of being locked out or stuck with no explanation.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Every error shown during testing appears immediately, states what went wrong, and gives at least one action the user can take to fix it.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} All user-initiated actions, including goal updates, portfolio transactions, account modifications, and fitness device connections, shall display a confirmation message, notification, or visual state change within 2 seconds of successful completion.

\textbf{Description:} The system shall simplify the process of connecting supported fitness devices and applications and maintain strong and reliable connections to these devices. 

\textbf{Rationale:} Integration with existing fitness technologies is a core project goal. Users should be able to link their existing devices without requiring technical expertise. Once connected, the system shall maintain a stable connection to ensure continuous data synchronization so that the user is not constantly required to re-establish the connection - as this will cause user frustration.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Users shall be able to perform a one-time action to connect a supported fitness data source through a guided setup workflow. Once connected the first time, the system shall not sever this connection more than three times per year without explicit user intervention.

\textbf{Rationale:} Consistency improves user familiarity and reduces the effort required to learn new functionality. 

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A design review checklist covering icons, terminology, colours, and interaction patterns finds no inconsistency between screens for the same action.

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:LF-STY-04}{LF-STY-04}\\

UHR-EOU-08\hypertarget{req:UHR-EOU-08}{} & 
Learnability & 
\textbf{Description:} The system shall require no more than 10 minutes for users to become proficient in performing core tasks and navigating the application. 

\textbf{Rationale:} Users should not require an excessive amount of time to learn and use the application effectively. Excessive time required to learn how to use the core functionalities of the application may lead to user frustration and disengagement.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} First-time users shall be able to make their first investment, set one fitness goal, and view fitness and portfolio performance metrics within 10 minutes from landing on the main screen after login.

\textbf{Description:} The system shall require no more than 5 minutes for former users to re-learn how to execute core tasks and navigating the application.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Former users with at least 1 month of consistent daily usage shall be able to re-learn how to manage at least one investment, manage at least one fitness goal, and view fitness and portfolio performance metrics within 5 minutes of returning to the system after 6+ months of inactivity.

PR-RFT-01\hypertarget{req:PR-RFT-01}{} &
Offline activity &
\textbf{Description:} Activity recorded while the phone is offline, or while the app is closed, shall be credited the next time the app syncs, for gaps of up to \const{OfflineSyncWindow}.

\textbf{Rationale:} Users exercise in places without signal and will not open the app every day. Losing that activity would discourage them.

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:FUNC-03}{FUNC-03}\\

PR-RFT-02\hypertarget{req:PR-RFT-02}{} &
Atomic writes &
\textbf{Description:} Every operation that changes balances, holdings, rewards, or progress shall either complete fully or be rolled back. A trade shall update both the currency balance and the holdings, at the price the user confirmed, or not happen at all.

\textbf{Rationale:} A write that fails halfway leaves balances, portfolios, or progress wrong. For example, a failed trade could take a user's currency without giving them the asset, or the other way around, and two simultaneous requests could produce duplicate rewards or a negative balance.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Tests that simulate a failure at each step of a multi-step write leave the data unchanged. When the connection or server is cut during a test trade, the balance and holdings afterwards match either the state before the trade or the state after it, and completed trades execute at the confirmed price. A stress test of 100 concurrent trade requests against one account leaves every operation either fully completed or fully rolled back, with no negative balance.

\textbf{Hazard Analysis:} SR12 (H7.2, H9.2)

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:FUNC-08}{FUNC-08}, \hyperlink{req:FUNC-09}{FUNC-09}, \hyperlink{req:FUNC-10}{FUNC-10}\\

PR-RFT-03\hypertarget{req:PR-RFT-03}{} &
Failing data sources &
\textbf{Description:} If a fitness data source is unavailable or returns invalid data, the app shall keep working with its other sources and credit no currency for the invalid data. A failed workout sync shall be retried automatically, and if it still fails, the app shall tell the user which source has a problem and why.

\textbf{Rationale:} Outages at Garmin or Health Connect are outside the team's control and should not make the whole app unusable. Users should find out about a failed sync from the app, not from a broken streak.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} With one source disabled or returning malformed data in testing, the app stays usable, retries the sync, then shows a message naming that source and the cause, and awards nothing for the bad data.

\textbf{Hazard Analysis:} SR4 (H3.1)

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:FUNC-03}{FUNC-03}, \hyperlink{req:MAS-MNT-04}{MAS-MNT-04}\\

\textbf{Rationale:} Users on poor connections should still be able to see their progress.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} With the backend stopped, the app opens, shows the last known balance and portfolio, and displays an offline notice.

SEC-ACC-01\hypertarget{req:SEC-ACC-01}{} &
Authenticated Access &
\textbf{Description:} Access granted when a user signs in (\hyperlink{req:FUNC-02}{FUNC-02}) shall be checked on every request for personal fitness data, portfolios, or transactions, and shall expire after \const{AccessExpiry}.

\textbf{Rationale:} Authentication protects personal data and virtual assets from unauthorized access. Expiring access limits the damage if a device or access credential is stolen.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Every protected request with missing, invalid, or expired access is rejected with \acr{HTTP}{Hypertext Transfer Protocol}{HTTP} status 401. After \const{AccessExpiry}, the app must refresh its access or the user must sign in again.

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:FUNC-02}{FUNC-02}, \hyperlink{req:COMP-LEG-02}{COMP-LEG-02}, \hyperlink{req:COMP-STD-07}{COMP-STD-07}\\

Role-Based Authorization &
\textbf{Description:} Role-based access control shall restrict users to their own records and limit administrative or evaluator access to authorized functions.

\textbf{Rationale:} Access restrictions prevent unauthorized modifications and cross-account data exposure.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} JUnit security tests shall confirm that attempts by standard users to access or modify another user's records return HTTP 403 in all tested cases.

\textbf{Hazard Analysis:} SR3 (H2.1, H8.1, H9.1)

Fitness Integration Authorization &
\textbf{Description:} Flexin' shall request only necessary read permissions for supported integrations, Android Health Connect and Garmin. Integration credentials shall be stored encrypted on the device, where other apps cannot read them.

\textbf{Rationale:} Secure credential handling protects external accounts and limits access to fitness data.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A security review confirms that integration tokens are stored only in encrypted device storage that other apps cannot read, and finds no credentials, tokens, or secrets in the project repository. 

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:FUNC-03}{FUNC-03}\\

SEC-ACC-04\hypertarget{req:SEC-ACC-04}{} &
Password and Sign-In Protection &
\textbf{Description:} Flexin' shall enforce a minimum password strength, offer optional multi-factor authentication through the user's email, and temporarily lock sign-in after repeated failed attempts. Users shall be able to reset their password through email.

\textbf{Rationale:} Weak passwords and unlimited sign-in attempts let attackers reach users' private data, while legitimate users must still be able to regain access to their accounts.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} Passwords shorter than \const{MinPasswordLength} characters or found on a common-password list shall be rejected. Sign-in to an account shall be locked for \const{LockoutDuration} after \const{LockoutAttempts} consecutive failed attempts. A user who enables multi-factor authentication shall be asked for an email code at each new sign-in, and a password reset email shall let a locked-out user sign in again.

\textbf{Hazard Analysis:} SR1 (H1.1, H1.2) 

\textbf{Related Requirements:} \hyperlink{req:FUNC-01}{FUNC-01}, \hyperlink{req:FUNC-02}{FUNC-02}, \hyperlink{req:SEC-AUD-02}{SEC-AUD-02}, \hyperlink{req:SEC-IMM-02}{SEC-IMM-02}\\

SEC-ACC-05\hypertarget{req:SEC-ACC-05}{} &
Session End and Device Unlinking &
\textbf{Description:} Flexin' shall end a user's session after a period of inactivity or when the user signs out, and shall unlink the user's fitness devices on sign-out.

\textbf{Rationale:} Sessions and device links left open on shared or lost devices let other people access an account or send their health data to it.

\textbf{Fit Criterion:} A session unused for \const{SessionTimeout} shall require the user to sign in again. After sign-out, the user's access and refresh tokens shall be rejected with HTTP 401, and no integration from that device shall be able to submit activity to the account.

\textbf{Hazard Analysis:} SR2 (H1.2, H11.2)

\section{Off-the-Shelf Solutions}
\subsection{Ready-Made Products}

The team looked at existing products that could meet the project's goals
without building something new. Each covers part of the problem, but none
connects fitness habits to learning how investments grow.

\begin{description}
  \item[Stock market simulators (e.g.\ Investopedia Simulator):] Free
  simulators give users virtual cash to trade real securities at market prices,
  and some let users compete in private groups. They teach investing well, but
  only to people who are already motivated to learn about it. The Problem
  Statement identifies young adults who are not yet interested as the group
  furthest behind, so a simulator alone does not reach them.

  \item[Step reward apps (e.g.\ Sweatcoin):] These apps turn steps into a
  virtual currency that can be spent on offers and products. They reward
  activity, but the currency is spent rather than invested, so they teach
  nothing about compounding.

  \item[Move-to-earn crypto apps (e.g.\ STEPN):] These reward exercise with
  cryptocurrency tokens that have real market value. They link exercise to
  money, but expose users to real financial loss. STEPN's reward token lost
  most of its value within months in 2022. This approach conflicts with
  constraint \hyperlink{req:SC-05}{SC-05} (simulated market only) and is not suitable.

  \item[Fitness trackers (e.g.\ Strava, Garmin Connect, Health Connect):]
  These record activity accurately but do not offer any financial component.
  \progname{} will use Health Connect and Garmin as data sources rather than
  replace them.
\end{description}

Since no ready-made product covers both halves of the problem, the team will
build \progname{}, reusing the components and ideas listed below.

    Native library for reading data from Android Health Connect.

  \end{itemize}

  \item[Mobile client:]\leavevmode

  \item[Backend:]\leavevmode

    \item Spring Security for authentication and access control, rather than

    \item Spring Data \acr{JPA}{Jakarta Persistence API}{JPA} for reading and writing the MySQL database.
  \end{itemize}

  \item[Historical market data:] A freely available historical data set for a

\subsection{Project Planning}

The team will follow an agile development process throughout the development of this solution, with a focus on iterative development and continuous feedback. The project will be divided into several sprints, each lasting two weeks. At the end of each sprint, the team will conduct a review to assess progress and make necessary adjustments. \subsection{Planning of the Development Phases}

The Flexin' project will be implemented incrementally through a series of development phases. Requirements are allocated to phases based on technical dependencies, implementation risk, stakeholder priority, and project milestones. Earlier phases focus on establishing the core platform infrastructure and fitness-to-investment workflow, while later phases introduce engagement features, usability enhancements, accessibility improvements, and compliance requirements. 

Each phase's deadline is the date its last task finishes in Table~\ref{tab:flexin_gantt_tasks}. Phases 1 to 3 are built in parallel between January 11 and February 21, 2027, so they share a deadline; their order is the order in which their features are started and prioritized, and the Rev 0 demo (February 1 to 12, 2027) shows them in progress. The requirements identified below represent the planned allocation at the time of writing. Requirement assignments and phase deadlines may be refined during development as new information becomes available through user testing, stakeholder feedback, and technical investigation.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{What went well while writing this deliverable?}

  Overall, the team maintained a high level of efficiency and productivity throughout the completion of this deliverable. Following our planning meetings, each team member took full accountability and stayed on top of their assigned tasks and sections, ensuring continuous forward momentum.

  We established strong alignment across the entire team in terms of collaboration expectations, communication cadence, and work quality. When technical or domain challenges arose, such as diagnosing and resolving Git merge issues or standardizing the formal phrasing and fit criteria for Volere requirements---the team collaborated effectively through ad-hoc calls and discussions to solve problems collectively.

  Additionally, the team successfully adapted to significant variations in individual availability and geographic dispersion. Despite several members taking vacations or traveling across distinct time zones (specifically with Noah traveling in Europe and Arjun located in Vancouver), the group maintained continuous communication through Microsoft Teams and GitHub. We adapted through asynchronous workflows, flexible scheduling, and clear issue tracking, ensuring that all sections progressed without disruption.

  \item \textbf{What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how did you resolve them?}

  The team experienced several key pain points during this deliverable cycle, which were addressed through proactive adjustments and teamwork:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Disparate Availability During Reading Week:} A large portion of the work occurred over Reading Week, during which team members experienced starkly differing availability schedules, vacations, and significant time-zone differences (Noah traveling in Europe and Arjun located in Vancouver). Finding mutual times for synchronous meetings proved difficult. We resolved this by relying primarily on asynchronous updates in Microsoft Teams, breaking work into modular GitHub issues, and holding focused, flexible meetings when schedules overlapped.
    \item \textbf{Git PR Overload and Branch Protection Bottlenecks:} Because team members worked concurrently across multiple feature branches to draft different sections of the extensive Volere document, a surge of Pull Requests arrived simultaneously right before the deadline. This led to Git merge conflicts, review backlogs, and pipeline friction. To resolve this under tight time constraints, we temporarily disabled GitHub branch protection rules and joined a live group call. We reviewed, tested, and merged all branches together in real time, verifying document integrity and preventing regressions.
    \item \textbf{Rushed Development Plan Integration:} Because branch merges took place until the day before the deadline, the downstream sections---notably the development plan and schedule---had to be completed last. Consequently, completing and polishing this section felt somewhat rushed. We resolved this by rallying as a group during our review call to finalize the section collaboratively, and we established an internal policy to enforce earlier merge freezes for future deliverables.
    \item \textbf{Estimating Performance Requirements Without Baselines:} Formulating quantitative, measurable performance requirements was challenging because we were not provided with baseline benchmarks or existing system metrics. Establishing realistic thresholds for latency, throughput, and synchronization required significant estimation. We resolved this by conducting a dedicated team walkthrough, researching performance benchmarks of comparable commercial fitness and personal finance applications, and collaboratively establishing reasonable, verifiable metrics.
  \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{How many of your requirements were inspired by speaking to your client(s) or their proxies (e.g. your peers, stakeholders, potential users)?}

  A substantial portion of our requirements---particularly our functional requirements---were directly inspired by stakeholder and client discussions. The team conducted two primary interviews:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item A formal mock interview with our client and supervisor, Chris Schankula, which helped establish the fundamental project scope, define educational learning goals, and clarify expectations around combining fitness habit-tracking with financial simulations.
    \item A dedicated user interview with a representative end-user (documented in the Appendix), which provided direct insight into real-world fitness routines, financial literacy levels, and motivational drivers.
  \end{enumerate}
  These interviews revealed that while fitness habits are often self-motivated through routine, financial education requires structured, bite-sized gamification (such as daily objective challenges inspired by Wordle) to maintain user engagement. These insights directly inspired functional requirements regarding daily interactive challenges, simplified financial glossaries, and automated tracking integrations. Because our team has chosen to produce a comprehensive User Interview report as our Custom Document deliverable, these early interviews were pivotal in grounding our requirements in genuine user and client needs.
