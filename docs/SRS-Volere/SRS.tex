\section{Performance Requirements}

Unless stated otherwise, the timing requirements in this section are measured
on a mid-range Android phone (one released within the last four years) with a
typical mobile data or Wi-Fi connection.

\subsection{Speed and Latency Requirements}

\begin{description}
  \item[PR-SL1: App launch]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The app shall show the home screen within 3 seconds of
    being opened from a closed state.
    \item[Rationale:] Users will mostly open the app for short check-ins, such
    as after a walk or workout. A slow start makes those check-ins less likely.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] Across 20 cold starts on the reference phone, the
    home screen appears in 3 seconds or less in at least 19 of them.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SL2: Response to input]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The app shall respond to a tap or navigation action
    within 1 second.
    \item[Rationale:] Supports the goal of a simple and delightful user
    experience. Delays longer than about a second make an app feel broken.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] At least 95\% of measured screen transitions and
    button responses complete within 1 second.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SL3: Trades]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] After a user confirms a buy or sell order, the updated
    holdings and currency balance shall be shown within 2 seconds.
    \item[Rationale:] Users need to see the result of a trade straight away to
    connect the action with its effect on their portfolio.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] At least 95\% of test trades show the updated
    portfolio within 2 seconds of confirmation.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SL4: Activity sync]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] When the app is opened, activity recorded since the
    last sync shall be read and turned into currency within 10 seconds.
    \item[Rationale:] The reward has to feel tied to the activity that earned
    it. The whole point of the product is to make a distant payoff feel
    immediate.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] In testing, the currency balance reflects all
    activity available in Health Connect within 10 seconds of opening the app.
  \end{description}
\end{description}

\subsection{Safety-Critical Requirements}

\progname{} does not control physical equipment, so none of its failures can
directly injure someone. The Hazard Analysis does identify ways the product
could encourage harm, and the requirements below address them. They will be
traced to the safety requirements in the Hazard Analysis once those are
finalized.

\begin{description}
  \item[PR-SC1: Daily earning cap]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The amount of currency a user can earn from activity
    in one day shall be capped.
    \item[Rationale:] Without a cap, the product would reward users for
    exercising past safe limits to earn more.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] No amount of activity recorded in a single day earns
    more than the daily cap.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SC2: No penalties for missed days]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The product shall not take away currency or holdings
    when a user misses a goal or a day of activity.
    \item[Rationale:] The Hazard Analysis lists shame over missed goals as a
    psychological hazard. Losing earned progress would make that worse, and
    could push users to exercise while injured or unwell.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] No feature of the product reduces a user's balance
    or holdings because of inactivity.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SC3: Simulated market is labelled]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] Every screen that shows prices, holdings, or returns
    shall state that the market is simulated and that results do not predict
    real returns.
    \item[Rationale:] The Hazard Analysis lists financial loss from users
    applying strategies from the app to real markets.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] A review of every market and portfolio screen finds
    the notice visible on each one.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SC4: Not financial advice]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The product shall state that its content is for
    education only and is not financial advice. The statement shall be shown
    during sign-up, where the user must acknowledge it before continuing, and
    shall stay available from the settings screen. Lessons, tips, and any other
    content that discusses investing shall not recommend specific real-world
    investments to a user.
    \item[Rationale:] The Hazard Analysis lists financial loss from users
    acting on what they learn in the app. \progname{} is not a licensed
    financial advisor, so users must understand that nothing in the app is
    personal advice about their real money.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] A new account cannot be created without
    acknowledging the statement, the statement can be reached from settings,
    and a review of all educational content finds no recommendations to buy
    or sell specific real-world investments.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SC5: Exercise injury warning]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The product shall warn users that physical activity
    carries a risk of injury, that they should exercise within their own
    limits, and that they should consult a doctor before starting a new
    exercise routine if they have health concerns. The product's terms of use
    shall state that users exercise at their own risk and that the
    \progname{} team is not responsible for any injury that happens while
    using the app. The user must acknowledge both during sign-up before
    continuing.
    \item[Rationale:] The product rewards physical activity, so some users
    will exercise more because of it. The Hazard Analysis assumes users only
    attempt exercises they are able to do safely, and places injuries from
    exercise outside the product's control. Users need to be told this
    directly. The responsibility statement is a legal matter as well as a
    safety one, so it should also be listed under the Legal Requirements in
    Compliance Requirements, and its exact wording should be checked against
    the course's guidance before release.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] A new account cannot be created without
    acknowledging the injury warning and the terms of use, and both can be
    reached from the settings screen at any time.
  \end{description}
\end{description}

\subsection{Precision or Accuracy Requirements}

\begin{description}
  \item[PR-PA1: Exact money values]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] Currency balances, prices, and trade amounts shall be
    stored and calculated as exact decimal values, not floating-point numbers,
    and shown to two decimal places.
    \item[Rationale:] Floating-point rounding errors build up over many trades
    and would show users balances that do not add up.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] After a scripted sequence of 1,000 trades, the
    stored balance matches the expected balance exactly.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-PA2: Consistent currency awards]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The currency awarded for a set of activity data shall
    follow the published conversion rules exactly, and the same data shall
    always earn the same amount.
    \item[Rationale:] Users must be able to trust that effort is rewarded
    fairly, and the team must be able to check that it is.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] For a set of test activity records, the awarded
    currency matches the amount calculated by hand from the conversion rules.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-PA3: No double counting]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] Activity reported by more than one source, such as the
    phone and a watch both counting the same steps, shall be counted only once.
    \item[Rationale:] Counting the same steps twice would over-reward users who
    own more devices, which the Hazard Analysis lists as a cause of poor
    learning.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] When the same walk is recorded by two sources, the
    currency earned equals the amount for one recording.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-PA4: Portfolio value]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The displayed portfolio value shall equal the sum of
    each holding's quantity times its current simulated price, plus uninvested
    currency.
    \item[Rationale:] The portfolio graph is the main tool for teaching
    compounding, so it has to be correct.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] For test portfolios, the displayed value matches the
    value calculated by hand to the cent.
  \end{description}
\end{description}

\subsection{Robustness or Fault-Tolerance Requirements}

\begin{description}
  \item[PR-RFT1: Offline activity]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] Activity recorded while the phone is offline, or while
    the app is closed, shall be credited the next time the app syncs, for gaps
    of up to 7 days.
    \item[Rationale:] Users exercise in places without signal and will not
    open the app every day. Losing that activity would discourage them.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] Activity recorded during a 7-day gap with no syncing
    is fully credited at the next sync.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-RFT2: Atomic trades]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] A trade shall either complete fully, updating both the
    currency balance and the holdings, or not happen at all.
    \item[Rationale:] A trade that fails halfway could take a user's currency
    without giving them the asset, or the other way around.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] When the connection or server is cut during a test
    trade, the balance and holdings afterwards match either the state before
    the trade or the state after it.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-RFT3: Failing data sources]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] If a fitness data source is unavailable or returns
    invalid data, the app shall keep working with its other sources, tell the
    user which source has a problem, and credit no currency for the invalid
    data.
    \item[Rationale:] Outages at Garmin, Strava, or Health Connect are outside
    the team's control and should not make the whole app unusable.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] With one source disabled or returning malformed data
    in testing, the app stays usable, shows a message naming that source, and
    awards nothing for the bad data.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-RFT4: Backend unavailable]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] If the backend cannot be reached, the app shall show
    the last data it received, marked as possibly out of date, instead of
    crashing or showing empty screens.
    \item[Rationale:] Users on poor connections should still be able to see
    their progress.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] With the backend stopped, the app opens, shows the
    last known balance and portfolio, and displays an offline notice.
  \end{description}
\end{description}

\subsection{Capacity Requirements}

\begin{description}
  \item[PR-C1: Users]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The system shall support at least 500 registered users,
    with at least 100 using the app at the same time, without breaking the
    speed requirements above.
    \item[Rationale:] This covers the expected load during user testing, the
    final demo, and the Capstone EXPO, where many people may try the app at
    once.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] A load test with 100 simulated concurrent users meets
    PR-SL2 and PR-SL3.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-C2: History]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The system shall store at least two years of activity
    and trade history for each user.
    \item[Rationale:] Compounding only shows up over long periods, so users
    need to be able to look back over their history.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] A test account loaded with two years of daily
    activity and weekly trades can display its full history, and its screens
    still meet PR-SL2.
  \end{description}
\end{description}

\subsection{Scalability or Extensibility Requirements}

\begin{description}
  \item[PR-SE1: Adding fitness sources]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] A new fitness data source shall be addable without
    changing the currency conversion logic.
    \item[Rationale:] The team expects to add device integrations over time,
    and the Problem Statement lists integration with existing fitness
    technologies as a goal.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] Adding a new source only requires changes inside the
    integration module.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SE2: Adding asset types]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] New types of assets shall be addable to the simulated
    market without changing the trading logic.
    \item[Rationale:] The product starts with stocks, bonds, and ETFs, and
    other asset types may be added later.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] Adding a new asset type only requires new market
    data and configuration, not changes to the buy and sell code.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SE3: More server capacity]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] The backend API shall keep no per-user state between
    requests, so more instances can be run if the number of users grows.
    \item[Rationale:] If the product continues after the capstone, as the
    business plan explores, it will need to serve more users than PR-C1.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] Two API instances running against the same database
    give the same results as one.
  \end{description}

  \item[PR-SE4: Other platforms]\leavevmode
  \begin{description}
    \item[Description:] Code that depends on Android-only features shall be
    kept separate from the rest of the client.
    \item[Rationale:] Support for iOS is a stretch goal, and keeping
    Android-specific code separate keeps that option open.
    \item[Fit Criterion:] All calls to Android-only APIs are in a clearly
    marked platform layer.
  \end{description}
\end{description}
